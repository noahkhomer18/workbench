# Retrospective

A retrospective is only useful if it changes how the next project runs. This skill follows the structure Derby and Larsen describe (set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close) and ends with a few changes that someone owns.

## When to use

- At the end of a project, after each phase of a long one, or after an incident.
- When the same problems come back from one project to the next.
- Not to rescue a project that is still running late: use `project-recovery`.

## Inputs

1. The plan, the final dates and costs, the status updates.
2. The people involved, and their perspectives.
3. What was promised to clients, partners or family.

## Method

1. **Set the stage**: the goal is to learn, not to blame. Assume that everyone did their best with what they knew at the time.
2. **Gather the facts**: a timeline of key events, planned versus actual (dates, budget, scope), and what each person noticed.
3. **Find what worked and what did not**, then dig into the causes of the main problems by asking "why" several times until the cause is something that can be changed.
4. **Decide on a few changes**: two or three, each with an owner and a date, and the way to check next time that it helped.
5. **Close the project**: confirm the deliverables are accepted, hand over to whoever maintains the result, archive the documents and the decision log, settle the last invoices, thank the people involved.
6. **Share the lessons** where the next project will find them.

## Output

```markdown
### Retrospective: <project or phase> (<date>)
Planned vs actual: dates … | budget … | scope …
Timeline: …
Worked well: … | Did not work: …
Causes: …
| Change | Owner | By | How we check |
|---|---|---|---|

### Closure checklist
Deliverables accepted | Handover done | Documents archived | Invoices settled | Thanks sent
```

## Quality bar

- Facts and the timeline come before opinions.
- Causes are dug into, and nobody is blamed.
- Two or three changes with owners and dates, not a long wish list.
- The project is formally closed: handover, documents, invoices, thanks.
- Written in the user's language, with the terms below.
