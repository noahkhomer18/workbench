# Weekly review

Projects stall when their next action is not written down anywhere. Allen's weekly review keeps every project moving with one short ritual; this skill runs it with the person and ends with a realistic week.

## When to use

- Once a week, at a fixed time, for everything the person is carrying.
- When someone feels overwhelmed or keeps forgetting things.
- Not to plan one big project in detail: use `work-breakdown` and `project-schedule`.

## Inputs

1. The list of current projects, at work and in personal life.
2. Loose notes, emails to process, promises made.
3. The calendar for the past and next two weeks, and the hours really available.

## Method

1. **Collect**: write down every open loop (tasks, promises, ideas, worries) until nothing is left in the person's head.
2. **Clarify each item**: is it actionable? If yes, what is the next physical action; if it takes two minutes, do it now; if someone else should do it, delegate it (`delegation-brief`); if not now, put it on a someday list.
3. **Review each project**: does it have a next action and a date? Is it still worth doing? Close, pause or drop the ones that are not.
4. **Look at the calendar**: two weeks back (follow-ups forgotten) and two weeks ahead (preparations needed).
5. **Choose three outcomes** for the coming week, not thirty tasks, and block time for them in the calendar.
6. **Check the load**: compare the planned work with the hours available; cut, delay or delegate until it fits.

## Output

```markdown
### Weekly review: week of <date>
Projects
| Project | Status | Next action | Date |
|---|---|---|---|

Three outcomes for this week: 1. … 2. … 3. …
Delegate: … | Drop or pause: … | Waiting for: …
Load check: … hours planned for … hours available
```

## Quality bar

- Every active project has a next action and a date.
- The week has three outcomes, and they fit the hours available.
- Something is dropped, paused or delegated when the load does not fit.
- Written in the user's language, with the terms below.
