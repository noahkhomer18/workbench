# Decision making

Hesitating costs time and energy, and deciding on gut feeling hides the reasons. This skill structures a choice so that it can be explained, checked and revisited if the facts change.

## When to use

- When a project is blocked by a choice between options.
- For decisions that cost money or are hard to reverse.
- Not for small, easily reversible choices: decide quickly and move on.

## Inputs

1. The decision to make, and its deadline.
2. The options already on the table.
3. What matters to the person or the team, and the facts available.

## Method

1. **Frame the problem** before comparing options: what exactly is being decided, by whom, by when, and what would make the decision good (Hammond, Keeney and Raiffa's PrOACT elements: problem, objectives, alternatives, consequences, tradeoffs).
2. **Classify the decision**: easy to reverse (decide fast) or hard to reverse (take the time, gather facts).
3. **Widen the options**: at least three real alternatives, including "do nothing" and a combination of two options.
4. **Set the criteria and their weights** before scoring the options, so that scoring does not bend the criteria.
5. **Score each option** in a decision matrix, with the facts behind each score. Facts that change (prices, rules, availability) come from current sources with a date.
6. **Check the biases** listed by Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony: falling in love with one option, only looking for confirming evidence, anchoring on the first number, sunk costs, overconfidence. Ask: what would have to be true for the second-best option to win?
7. **Decide and record**: the decision, the reasons, what would make us revisit it, who was consulted, and the date.

## Output

## Quality bar

- At least three real options, criteria weighted before scoring.
- Every score rests on a fact or a labeled assumption.
- Biases are checked explicitly, and the decision record says when to revisit it.
- The tool supports the decision; the person still decides.
- Written in the user's language, with the terms below.
