# Meeting toolkit

Rogelberg's research shows that meetings can be fixed rather than abolished: a clear purpose, the right people, an agenda of questions and a written follow-up. This skill prepares and closes any meeting that way.

## When to use

- Before a meeting: to decide whether it is needed and to write the agenda.
- After a meeting: to turn notes into minutes with decisions and actions.
- Not to report progress that could be read: use `status-update`.

## Inputs

1. The purpose of the meeting and the decisions or questions on the table.
2. The participants and their roles.
3. For minutes: the raw notes or the transcript.

## Method

1. **Check that a meeting is needed**: if the goal is only to share information, send a written update instead; meet to decide, solve or align.
2. **Write the agenda as questions**, each with an owner, a time box and the expected outcome (decide, choose, give input). Put the most important item first.
3. **Invite only the people needed** for each decision; others get the minutes.
4. **Prepare the material** to read before the meeting (options, figures, a recommendation) and send it with the agenda.
5. **Set roles**: who leads, who keeps time, who writes the decisions and actions.
6. **Close with a check**: read back the decisions and the actions with owner and date before people leave.
7. **Send the minutes within 24 hours**: decisions, actions (owner and date), open questions, next meeting. Keep a decision log for the project.

## Output

```markdown
### Agenda: <meeting> (<date>, <duration>)
Purpose: … | Read before: …
| Time | Question | Owner | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|

### Minutes: <meeting> (<date>)
Decisions: 1. …
| Action | Owner | Due date |
|---|---|---|
Open questions: … | Next meeting: …
```

## Quality bar

- Every agenda item is a question with an expected outcome and a time box.
- Minutes list decisions and actions with owners and dates, not a transcript.
- Information-only meetings are replaced by a written update when possible.
- Written in the user's language, with the terms below.
